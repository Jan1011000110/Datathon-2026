THE DECISION RULE

Discharge up to a quantile of the forecast

Discharge the d-th kW only if

w · η · P(net load > d) > v

w this hour’s signal · η one-way efficiency · v what a stored kWh is worth after this hour, from the plan.

At 04:00 and 19:00 the load is steady and the answer stays within a few percent of the forecast.

At 13:00, with solar uncertain, it ranges from 0 to 2 times the forecast. A fixed 10 % haircut cannot do both.

This is the rule the plan produces for the hour being decided. Discharge one more kilowatt only if what it earns now, times the chance that the campus actually uses it, is more than what that energy is worth later. The left plot is a weekday at one in the afternoon. The point forecast of net load is 443 kilowatts. If a stored kilowatt-hour is worth 90 percent as much later, the agent discharges only 214, the part that is almost certain to be used. If it is worth only 25 percent later, it discharges 523, beyond the forecast. The right plot shows why a fixed safety margin is wrong: at four in the morning or seven in the evening the load is steady and the best discharge is the forecast itself; at one in the afternoon, when solar is uncertain, it goes from zero to twice the forecast. Charging is the mirror image.
