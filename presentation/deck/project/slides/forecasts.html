Three forecasters, nothing fancy

Campus load

Model

Rules for the meter first, then LightGBM (300 trees, 15 leaves, Huber loss) on the change from the last reading.

28 inputs

Hour and weekday; the last three readings; the same hour yesterday and last week; the mean of the last 3 days of the same type; a holiday index from today’s readings; temperature.

Result

Error for the hour we’re deciding: 38 → 20 kW

Solar output

Model

Clear-sky envelope (90th percentile of train output, ±12 days) × clearness. One ridge regression per horizon predicts the clearness.

Inputs

Clearness of the last hour, of today so far, of yesterday and of the same hour on the last day seen; panel minus air temperature.

Result

91 → 85 kW. We tried LightGBM here too: 151 kW, so we threw it out

Price and CO₂, +3 to +36 h

Model

One ridge regression per horizon and per signal, on the difference from an average day (month, day type, hour).

Inputs

The last 48 known differences, the other signal’s last 12, the average-day value at the target hour. Nudged every hour by the newest error we see.

Result

CO₂ error at +3 h: 7.8 → 4.6 g/kWh. Price: 1.18 → 0.74 c€/kWh

What we predict and with what. Load: the meter has regimes, so rules come first. It doesn't update at night, and at six in the morning it jumps by an amount that depends on the weekday. On top of that, a LightGBM with 300 small trees and 28 inputs predicts the change from the last reading. The inputs are things like the last three readings, the same hour yesterday and last week, the average of the last three days of the same type, and a holiday index it estimates from today's readings. That halves the error for the hour we're deciding, from 38 to 20 kilowatts. Solar is more physical: a clear-sky curve for each day of the year, times a clearness factor from a ridge regression. We tried LightGBM there too and it was much worse, so out it went. Price and CO2 past three hours: one ridge regression per horizon, on the difference from an average day. All these errors are on validation weeks with the models fitted outside them, compared with a plain average-profile forecast.
