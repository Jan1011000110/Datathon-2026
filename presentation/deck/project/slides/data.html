ANALYSIS OF THE DATA

What the data told us

CO₂ here is the share of emitting plants

The CO₂ column is exactly 0.37 × the share of emitting generation in the peninsular mix, on all 6,768 training rows. The battery is avoiding the hours when those plants run.

The campus, not the battery, limits discharge

In 87 % of the hours when the agent discharges, the battery could deliver more than the campus draws. The load of the current hour is what matters.

The meter is silent at night

From 23:00 to 05:00 the load reading does not update, so night decisions cannot lean on the last value. A probe confirmed the same on the server.

A better forecast is not a better score

Four times a more accurate forecast failed to raise the score. We judged every change by the score of the whole control loop, never by forecast error.

Four findings shaped the design. First, the CO2 signal is not a measurement: it is exactly 0.37 times the share of emitting generation, on every training row. Second, the battery is larger than the campus can absorb: in 87 percent of discharge hours the limit is the load, so forecasting the current hour's load matters more than forecasting far ahead. Third, the load meter does not update at night, which we confirmed on the server with a probe. Fourth, and the one that cost us time: four times we built a more accurate forecaster and the score did not move. From then on every change was judged by the closed-loop score.
