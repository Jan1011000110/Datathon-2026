HOW THE BATTERY IS ALLOCATED

A linear programme, solved as an exact DP

Objective. Minimise grid import over the next 36 hours, each kWh weighted by w = CO₂ + 0.27 × price. Limits: 1,500 kW, 6,000 kWh, 95 % efficiency each way. Energy sent to the grid earns nothing.

Solver. The value of stored energy is convex and piecewise linear, so we keep only its slope: a sorted list of (value per kWh, width). One hour back in time is a merge of two sorted lists. No grid over the state of charge.

Uncertainty. The load and solar error distributions are combined into 9 equally likely net-load scenarios for each of the next 12 hours. The value of stored energy is averaged half-and-half between the signal forecast and 20 sampled error paths.

Control. Re-planned every hour with the newest readings; only the first command is applied.

How is the battery allocated? It is a linear programme: minimise the grid import of the next 36 hours, each kilowatt-hour weighted by the score signal, which is CO2 plus 0.27 times the price, subject to the power, capacity and efficiency limits. We started with scipy's LP solver and then replaced it. The value of the energy in the battery is a convex, piecewise-linear function of the state of charge, so it is enough to keep its slope, a short sorted list. Going one hour back in time is a merge of two sorted lists. That is an exact dynamic programme: it matches the LP to eleven decimal places on 4,500 cases and is about twenty times faster, with numpy only. The speed is what lets us plan under uncertainty: nine scenarios of net load for each of the next twelve hours, built from the error distributions of the load and solar forecasters, and twenty error paths of the price and CO2 signal sampled from held-out weeks. Planning on the distribution instead of the point forecast was the largest single gain, 0.71 points with the same forecasts.
