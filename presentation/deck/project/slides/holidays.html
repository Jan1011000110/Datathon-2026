How three Barcelona holidays fooled the agent

  • The test weeks are the last seven days of each month. Our validation weeks are days 1 to 21, so they never see a month end.
  • Three test weeks start on a holiday: La Mercè, Whit Monday and Sant Joan.
  • On a holiday the campus draws about half its usual load.
  • But prices keep their weekday shape on those days, so the agent was buying energy overnight for a campus that never showed up.

We predicted +0.20. The server said

+0.23

on the public board, 23.32 → 23.55, from three dates typed into the load forecaster.

Outside those three weeks the agent’s commands are identical to before, so nothing else can explain the jump.

Here's the holiday one in detail, because it's the result we like most. Our validation weeks are days 1 to 21 of each month, but the test weeks are the last seven days. And if you look at a calendar, three of those test weeks start on a holiday: La Mercè on 24 September, Whit Monday on 25 May and Sant Joan on 24 June. On a holiday the campus uses about half the usual energy. But on these local and regional holidays the electricity price keeps its normal weekday shape. So the agent did the sensible weekday thing: buy cheap energy overnight for the morning. And then nobody came. We typed the three dates into the load forecaster, predicted plus 0.20, and the server gave plus 0.23. It also told us something we couldn't check from the data: yes, the campus really closes on Barcelona's local holidays.
